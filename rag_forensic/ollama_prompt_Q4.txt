FIFO (First-In-First-Out), LIFO (Last-In-First-Out), and Weighted Average are three common costing methods used in inventory systems.

**FIFO (First-In-First-Out)**: This method assumes that the first items received or produced are the first ones sold or consumed. In this approach, the cost of the oldest items is matched with the revenue generated from their sale. FIFO is often used when there is a high volume of inventory turnover and it's easier to track the order in which items were received.

**LIFO (Last-In-First-Out)**: This method assumes that the last items received or produced are the first ones sold or consumed. In this approach, the cost of the most recent items is matched with the revenue generated from their sale. LIFO is often used when there is a low volume of inventory turnover and it's easier to track the order in which items were received.

**Weighted Average**: This method calculates the average cost of all items in stock, taking into account both the quantity and value of each item. The weighted average cost is then matched with the revenue generated from the sale of those items. Weighted Average is often used when there are multiple purchases or productions of the same item at different prices.
